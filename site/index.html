This is the part nobody is building
The wedge

The payout is the easy half.

A US business paying a contractor in Manila or Lagos picks between a wire that costs tens of dollars and takes days, or a patchwork of consumer apps that were never meant for payroll. Remi makes that one sentence and one Face ID prompt. The money arrives in about a second and costs about a cent to move.

That part is genuinely better, and it is not the interesting part. Fast stablecoin payroll is a crowded idea — Deel, Rise, Request Finance and Toku all ship a version of it. Being the cleanest one wins a category, not a company.

What nobody ships is the other side of the transfer. The contractor who received that $400 has no portable evidence it happened. Their bank statement is in the wrong currency, their client list lives in three different dashboards, and a screenshot proves nothing. So they are invisible to every landlord, lender and visa officer who asks a simple question: what do you earn?

Remi answers that question as a byproduct of getting paid.

The product

Every payout writes an income record the worker owns.

Each payout emits a signed record carrying the amount, the date, the client and the category. The business sees it as a receipt and a bookkeeping entry. The contractor sees it as one line in a verifiable earnings history that follows them across every client they ever invoice.

This is what turns a one-sided tool into a network. The contractor now has a reason to want their other clients on Remi, because a Passport with three clients on it is worth more than one with a single client. The business brings the first payout; the worker brings the next business.

Privacy

One passkey. Two keys. The worker holds the second one.

A public income history attached to a named person would be a worse product than a bank statement, not a better one. So the record and the identity are split apart, and the worker keeps the key that joins them.

A passkey can derive more than one key. Under one salt it derives the account that gets paid. Under a different salt it derives an encryption key that never touches a payout.

The key that gets paid

salt: mera default

Signs payouts. Derived fresh for each confirmation and zeroed the moment it is used. This is the account the money moves through.

The key that unlocks the record

salt: sha256("remi.passport.v1")

Encrypts names, countries and client identities client-side. Never signs anything. Without it, the settled records are amounts and dates attached to nobody.

both derived from the same Face ID · neither is ever stored

So disclosure becomes a choice instead of a leak. Maria sends her landlord a link that decrypts one view of her history. The landlord verifies the amounts settled without learning who her clients are. Nobody — not Remi, not her clients, not a server — can read the book she has not shared.

Sign in on a new phone with the same Face ID and both keys come back. No seed phrase, no backup file, no recovery email.

The obvious question

Why this needs a chain at all.

Moving a stablecoin and emitting an event works on any chain, and "settlement is fast" is true of several. That is not the argument.

Remi does not use the chain to move money. It uses the chain so that no single employer owns a worker's income history. If these records live in Remi's database, they are Remi's records and the worker is a tenant. If they live as public state, a lender can underwrite them without Remi's permission, a competitor can read them without Remi's cooperation, and the worker keeps them if Remi disappears. Portability is the entire product, and portability is what a shared ledger is actually for.

That choice has a cost, and the cost is what picks the chain. A credential is only worth anything if it is complete, which means every payout must be written, not just the large ones — weekly payouts, across millions of workers, each one a state write rather than a transfer. That is a high-frequency write workload, and writes have to stay close to free or the product quietly starts skipping records. Monad is the chain where per-payout writes at that volume stay cheap, and where the two pieces Remi depends on — AUSD for settlement and passkey accounts with no custody backend — are already native.

The end state is underwriting. A worker with five months of verified, multi-client income is someone you can advance against, and nothing in the traditional system can see them. Remi does not build lending this weekend. It builds the credential lending needs and shows the number it produces.
